# Weatherman Chan: content style guide

**Audience:** secondary students (US grades 6–12, Canada), often exploring on their own.

**Tone:** friendly yet professional. Serious about the science; never patronizing.

## Principles
1. **Scaffold every concept.** Order: observe (live data) → name it (vocabulary) → explain why (mechanism) → apply (prediction or decision) → connect (society, food, culture, business).
2. **Introduce jargon carefully.** Use the plain phrase first, then the term in bold with a one-line definition. Reuse the term consistently afterward. Example: "air pressing down on us, called **atmospheric pressure**".
3. **Show, then tell.** Each idea gets a live-data view or an animation before the paragraph.
4. **Ask before answering.** Prompt a prediction ("What do you expect the wind to do when pressure drops?") before revealing the explanation.
5. **Be honest about uncertainty.** Forecasts are probabilities and model estimates. Say so, and use it as a teaching moment.
6. **Units.** Always show the unit; offer metric and imperial. Explain conversions when they matter.
7. **Sources.** Cite data sources and link to agencies (NOAA, NWS, Environment Canada) for official warnings.

## Accessibility
- Every animation has pause/step controls and a text alternative.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Do not rely on color alone to carry meaning.
- Keep reading level appropriate; define terms on first use.

## Privacy
No accounts, analytics, ads, or third-party trackers. Preferences are kept in the browser only.
